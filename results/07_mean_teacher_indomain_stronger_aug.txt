Experiment: Mean Teacher SSL, in-domain PUMA unlabeled data, with STRONGER consistency-branch
augmentation (affine warp + wider jitter), re-run for fair comparison against result 06.
Data: 15 labeled / 165 unlabeled (both PUMA) / 25 held-out val — same split as original run.
Config: 60 epochs, batch_size=2, lr=5e-5, EMA decay=0.99.
Consistency-branch augmentation: affine warp (±30 deg rotation, ±10% translation via
  grid_sample), brightness/contrast jitter ±40%, gaussian noise std=0.08.
  (Original run used mild brightness/contrast ±20% + noise std=0.05, no spatial warp.)

RESULT: Best val_dice = 0.8323

Comparison (15 labeled, in-domain PUMA unlabeled, isolating augmentation strength only):
  Mild augmentation (original run):    0.8661
  Strong augmentation (this run):      0.8323

CONCLUSION: Strengthening the consistency-branch augmentation made in-domain Mean Teacher
WORSE, not better. Training was visibly rockier from epoch 1 and never converged as smoothly
as the mild-augmentation run. Most likely explanation: with only 15 labeled anchors, demanding
consistency under a ±30-degree rotation + wide color jitter is too aggressive a transform for
the model to learn to be invariant to reliably at this label count — the strong perturbation
adds more instability than useful signal. This is the opposite of what was intended (the change
was meant to give consistency regularization "more to be robust against"); instead it mostly
made the consistency target harder to hit correctly. Combined with result 06, this shows
augmentation strength and unlabeled-data domain have independent, sometimes opposing effects
on Mean Teacher SSL performance — conflating them in a single before/after comparison would
have been misleading.
