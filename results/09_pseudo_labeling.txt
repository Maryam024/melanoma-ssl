Experiment: Confidence-thresholded pseudo-labeling (self-training), the simplest standard SSL
baseline. Two-stage: (1) train on labeled data only, (2) generate pseudo-masks on unlabeled
data, keep only images where >=80% of pixels exceed a 0.9 confidence threshold, retrain on
labeled + kept pseudo-labeled data combined.
Data: 15 labeled / 165 unlabeled / 25 held-out val.
Config: Stage 1 = 40 epochs labeled-only. Confidence threshold = 0.9 (pixel-level),
  image kept if >=80% of its pixels are confident.

Stage 1 (labeled-only) done.
Kept 0 / 165 unlabeled images as confident pseudo-labels.
Stage 2 not run (no pseudo-labeled data available).

RESULT: No usable result — 0 unlabeled images passed the confidence filter.

CONCLUSION: At 15 labeled images, the Stage-1 model is not confident enough (per-pixel) across
enough of any unlabeled image to pass the threshold. Confidence-thresholded self-training
assumes a baseline level of model reliability to bootstrap from; that assumption does not hold
at this label count. This is treated as a genuine negative result, not a bug — lowering the
confidence threshold to force pseudo-labels through was deliberately not done, since accepting
low-confidence self-generated labels risks reinforcing the model's own errors rather than
providing new information. Pseudo-labeling was not re-tested at 40 labeled images; based on the
Stage-1 confidence pattern here, it is plausible it would still under-produce confident labels,
though this was not directly verified.
