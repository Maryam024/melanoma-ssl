Experiment: Simple baseline model on the second (TCGA-SKCM) dataset, as requested by supervisor.
Task: primary vs. metastatic melanoma tissue classification at the patch level.
Rationale: TCGA-SKCM patches have no nuclei masks (unlike PUMA), so segmentation was not
possible here. Sample-type labels already encoded in each slide's TCGA barcode (01 = primary,
06 = metastatic) were used instead, requiring no manual annotation.
Model: ResNet18 (ImageNet-pretrained), binary classification head.

=== Attempt 1: 8 slides (3 primary, 5 metastatic), random 1-slide holdout ===
Result: best val_acc = 0.7750 -- INVALID. The held-out slide happened to be 100% primary,
so the validation set contained zero metastatic examples. Confusion matrix confirmed this
(second row entirely zero). Number discarded.

=== Attempt 2: 8 slides, stratified split (1 primary + 1 metastatic held out) ===
Val patches: 40 primary / 40 metastatic (balanced).
Result: best val_acc = 0.5250. Confusion matrix: [[0 40],[0 40]] -- model predicted the
majority training class for every validation patch. No real discrimination learned.

=== Attempt 3: 8 slides, same split, with train-time augmentation added ===
(flip, color jitter) to reduce reliance on slide-specific staining.
Result: best val_acc = 0.5375, still collapsed to one-class prediction on the confusion
matrix. Augmentation did not change the outcome.

=== Attempt 4 (final): 30 slides (15 primary, 15 metastatic), stratified split ===
6 slides held out (2 val slides originally at 8-slide scale insufficient; scaled up).
Train patches: 960 | Val patches: 240 (120 primary / 120 metastatic, balanced).

Epoch 1/20  | val_acc=0.5250
Epoch 7/20  | val_acc=0.5583  <- best
Epoch 10/20 | val_acc=0.3833
Epoch 15/20 | val_acc=0.3750
Epoch 20/20 | val_acc=0.4958

Best val_acc: 0.5583
Confusion matrix at best epoch: [[40 80],[26 94]]

RESULT: Predictions are no longer collapsed to a single class (unlike attempts 2-3), but
val_acc fluctuates near chance level (0.50 for a balanced binary task) throughout all 20
epochs with no stable upward trend. Training loss dropped smoothly and normally throughout,
indicating the model fits the training slides fine but does not generalize to held-out slides.

DIAGNOSIS: With only 30 total slides, the model most likely learns slide-specific staining
and scanner characteristics rather than a generalizable biological signal. This is a known
challenge in digital pathology with small slide counts. There is also a plausible biological
explanation: primary and metastatic melanoma are the same cell type, and prior work has noted
structural similarity between primary and metastatic tumors in whole-slide histology, so a
single small patch may not carry a reliably distinguishing signal without broader anatomical
or slide-level context.

CONCLUSION: This is reported as the baseline result for the TCGA-SKCM dataset. Further
tuning was not pursued past this point, since two independent fixes (stratified split,
augmentation) and a 4x increase in slide count did not change the qualitative outcome.
Likely requires substantially more slides (tens to hundreds per class) than were available
in this scope to succeed.
