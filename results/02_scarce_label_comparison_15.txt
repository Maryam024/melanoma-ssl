Experiment: Semi-supervised learning comparison at extreme label scarcity
Data: 15 labeled / 165 unlabeled / 25 held-out val (all from PUMA, same distribution)
Config: 60 epochs, batch_size=2, mixed precision, lr=5e-5 (fine-tuning stage)

--- (a) Supervised-only, random init, 15 labeled examples ---
Final epochs converge to best val_dice = 0.8702

--- (b) Mean Teacher SSL (consistency regularization), 15 labeled + 165 unlabeled ---
Student trained with supervised loss (15 labeled) + consistency loss vs.
EMA teacher (165 unlabeled, color-jitter + noise as student-side augmentation)
Best val_dice = 0.8661

--- (c) Autoencoder-pretrained encoder, single learning rate (5e-5) for full model ---
Encoder pretrained via image reconstruction on all 180 images (15+165, no masks),
then transplanted into segmentation UNet, fine-tuned on 15 labeled examples only.
Best val_dice = 0.7692

--- (d) Autoencoder-pretrained encoder, DIFFERENTIAL learning rates ---
Same pretrained encoder as (c), but fine-tuned with encoder_lr=1e-5 (gentle)
and decoder_lr=3e-4 (fast, since decoder started randomly initialized).
Best val_dice = 0.8521

=== SUMMARY (15 labeled examples) ===
Supervised-only, random init:               0.8702  (best)
Autoencoder-pretrained, differential lr:    0.8521
Mean Teacher SSL (consistency):             0.8661
Autoencoder-pretrained, single lr:          0.7692  (worst)

CONCLUSION: Neither SSL method tested improved over plain supervised training
in this setup. The autoencoder's initial underperformance was substantially
(not fully) explained by a fine-tuning learning-rate mismatch between the
pretrained encoder and randomly-initialized decoder. Most likely root cause:
the unlabeled pool is drawn from the same distribution as the labeled data
(same dataset, scanner, staining), giving consistency- and reconstruction-based
methods little genuinely new information to exploit. Real domain diversity
(e.g. out-of-institution unlabeled WSIs) is hypothesized to be necessary for
these methods to show benefit — proposed as next step.
